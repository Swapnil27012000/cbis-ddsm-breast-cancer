4. SCIENTIFIC OBSERVATIONS: CONTRAST ENHANCEMENT (STAGE 6)
--------------------------------------------------
- Global Histogram Equalization (HistEq):
  * Visibly spreads global dynamic range across all 256 intensity levels.
  * Severely amplifies low-level scanner sensor noise in dark non-breast background areas.
  * Causes unnatural saturation and overexposure in dense glandular regions, potentially
    obscuring subtle architectural distortions or calcification morphology.
- Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization (CLAHE, clip=2.0, tileGrid=8x8):
  * Adaptively enhances local glandular contrast without severe global over-amplification.
  * Clip limit of 2.0 effectively limits background noise enhancement relative to Global HistEq.
  * Preserves boundary gradients between fatty and fibroglandular tissues.

5. SCIENTIFIC OBSERVATIONS: SHARPENING (STAGE 7)
--------------------------------------------------
- Unsharp Masking (sigma=1.0, amount=1.0, threshold=0):
  * Produces visible edge crispness along glandular borders and skin margins.
  * In Global HistEq images, sharpening significantly compounds noise amplification,
    increasing strong edge percentage from 19.34% to 27.28% and generating granular grain.
  * In CLAHE images, sharpening sharpens parenchymal textures while maintaining tolerable
    saturation levels (near-one saturation remains low at ~1.46%).
  * Absolute difference maps (|Sharpened - Control|) confirm that modifications are strictly
    localized to structural edges; homogeneous tissue and background remain largely unaffected.

6. SCIENTIFIC OBSERVATIONS: SYNTHETIC NOISE MODELS (STAGE 8)
--------------------------------------------------
- Gaussian Noise (sigma=0.03): Uniform additive degradation across both tissue and dark background.
- Salt & Pepper Noise (1%): Discrete white and black impulse dropouts causing severe visual speckling.
- Multiplicative Speckle (sigma=0.05): Noise amplitude scales proportionally with tissue intensity;
  background air remains clean, while dense glandular zones exhibit noticeable scattering.
- Poisson Noise (peak=30): Simulates photon counting quantum shot noise; exhibits signal-dependent
  coarseness with significant entropy drop in dark areas.
- Mixed Poisson-Gaussian: Combines quantum shot fluctuations with electronic thermal floor;
  constitutes the most visually severe compound degradation.

7. SCIENTIFIC OBSERVATIONS: DENOISING METHODS (STAGE 9)
--------------------------------------------------
- Median & Adaptive Median Filters:
  * Outstanding visual elimination of Salt & Pepper impulse spikes (PSNR jumps to 49.6-52.8 dB).
  * Adaptive Median dynamically expands window size (3x3 to 7x7), preserving finer edges
    better than standard 3x3 median on non-impulse structures.
- Gaussian Spatial Smoothing (sigma=1.0, kernel=5x5):
  * Smooths high-frequency thermal noise effectively (PSNR ~38.5 dB on Gaussian noise).
  * Causes indiscriminate blurring of sharp microcalcification borders and fine trabeculae.
- Wiener & Bilateral Filters:
  * Bilateral filter preserves tissue-air and fibroglandular boundaries well (edge-preserving).
  * Wiener adaptive MMSE provides well-balanced smoothing on stationary Gaussian noise.
- Non-Local Means (NLM):
  * High patch similarity in homogeneous areas; excellent preservation of background cleanliness.
  * Computationally intensive; tends to oversmooth irregular textured margins on mixed noise.
- Anscombe-Wiener:
  * Anscombe algebraic variance stabilization transforms Poisson noise into near-Gaussian,
    producing visibly superior restoration on quantum shot models.
- Kuan Filter:
  * Local statistics adapt well to multiplicative speckle noise, retaining glandular contrast.

8. TRIANGULATION: VISUAL APPEARANCE vs QUANTITATIVE METRICS (STAGE 10)
--------------------------------------------------
- Overall Agreement:
  * Quantitative metrics (PSNR, SSIM, MSE) strongly corroborate visual findings on impulse noise:
    Adaptive Median and Median achieve top PSNR (52.8 dB, 49.6 dB) and SSIM (0.995, 0.991) on Salt & Pepper.
  * Gaussian filter scores high whole-image PSNR on Gaussian noise (38.5 dB), but magnified crops
    reveal visible edge softening that a single global metric does not fully penalize.
  * On Speckle noise, all denoisers achieve high PSNR (>38 dB) and SSIM (>0.91), consistent with
    speckle being absent from the dark background air.
- Divergences & Metric Limitations:
  * Whole-image metrics are heavily influenced by the large exterior black air region (40-60% of pixels).
  * When comparing Whole-Image vs Foreground-Only metrics, Foreground PSNR is consistently 1.5 to 2.5 dB
    lower, demonstrating that whole-image evaluations overestimate perceived restoration fidelity.
  * Comparison C (Noisy vs Denoised) must NEVER be mistaken for restoration fidelity:
    Filters that perform minimal smoothing (e.g., NLM on Poisson) score very high ND-PSNR (43.4 dB)
    simply because they altered the noisy input very little, not because they recovered the clean image.
